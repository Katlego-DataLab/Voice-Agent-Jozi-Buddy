Party Positions Compared: 2026 Local Government Manifestos
A descriptive comparison of what six parties say they will do, drawn from their own manifestos. It describes stated positions only and makes no judgement on whether they will work or who to vote for.
Read this first
• RISE Mzansi is the January 2024 national manifesto, not a local one. Its commitments are national-level and are marked "(national)".
• Page numbers are PDF pages. They match printed numbers for five documents, but the EFF's printed numbers are 2 lower (PDF p.42 prints as 40).
• Education and healthcare are mostly provincial functions, so local manifestos say little on them. Where a party has no dedicated commitment, this is stated.
• The topic sections were read closely, but not every page of the 60 to 80 page documents. Check each line against its page before reusing it.
Water
• ActionSA: Repair and replace pipes, pumps and reservoirs, stop leaks, and plan for water scarcity. Replace contracted tankers with municipal capacity (p.14).
• ANC: Repair ageing infrastructure, develop alternative sources, and reduce non-revenue water below 30% in every metro (pp.22, 24).
• DA: Ringfence water revenue for water infrastructure, use public-private partnerships, install smart meters, and publish leak reports (pp.16–17).
• EFF: Fix leaks within a day of being reported, set up municipal plumbing teams, and write off water debt from incorrect billing (pp.52–53).
• RISE (national): Repair major water and flood infrastructure, and pipe potable water to every home (pp.34, 44).
• VF Plus: Prioritise sewage plants and water infrastructure, use technology to detect leaks, and upgrade existing infrastructure before building new (p.17).
Electricity
• ActionSA: Maintain and replace substations, tackle theft and illegal connections, and seek alternative producers (p.16).
• ANC: Upgrade networks, expand smart metering, raise Free Basic Electricity, and build municipal self-generation capacity (p.22).
• DA: Ringfence electricity revenue, let municipalities buy and sell their own power, and pay residents for surplus solar fed back to the grid (pp.22–23).
• EFF: Treat electricity as a public good, stop extra prepaid charges, restore municipal control of billing, and develop municipal renewable generation (pp.42–43).
• RISE (national): Expand solar and decentralised energy, with free daytime electricity for low-income households once the crisis is resolved (p.54).
• VF Plus: Install tamper-proof smart and area meters, encourage private generation, and offer rebates for renewables (pp.15, 20–21).
Infrastructure and service delivery (roads, refuse, maintenance)
• ActionSA: Maintain before things break, protect maintenance budgets, set pothole repair standards, and run scheduled refuse collection (pp.15, 18).
• ANC: Spend at least 8% of asset value on maintenance and publish maintenance plans in every municipality (pp.21, 24).
• DA: Meet the 8% maintenance benchmark and use public-private partnerships and pre-approved contractor panels (p.12).
• EFF: Hire engineers and artisans directly, and insource refuse collection (pp.26, 57).
• RISE (national): Repair and expand network infrastructure and transport, with little municipal detail (pp.44, 48).
• VF Plus: Spend infrastructure grants in full, meet the 8% benchmark, and fund critical infrastructure before stadiums (p.17).
Jobs and economic growth
• ActionSA: Cut red tape, publish approval turnaround standards, and give investors a single entry point (pp.47–48).
• ANC: Make municipalities investment-ready, expand public employment programmes, and set local procurement targets for women, youth and disabled-owned firms (pp.27–29).
• DA: Offer investment-ready toolkits, introduce "silence-is-consent" permits, and replace B-BBEE with a poverty-based model (pp.44–45).
• EFF: Insource services for permanent jobs, set up municipal artisan units, and allocate at least 80% of procurement to local SMMEs and cooperatives (pp.29–30).
• RISE (national): Fund small, black-owned and women-owned enterprises, use preferential procurement, and place failing municipalities in major economic nodes under administration (pp.32, 38).
• VF Plus: Municipalities should enable private-sector job creation rather than create jobs themselves, and remove red tape (pp.23–24).
Corruption, accountability and governance
• ActionSA: Digital audit trails, public tender data, conflict-of-interest declarations and supplier debarment (pp.24, 35).
• ANC: Fill municipal manager and CFO posts within 120 days, require councillor performance agreements with recall for persistent failure, and make coalition agreements public (pp.45–47).
• DA: End cadre deployment, make accounting officers personally repay losses, and disclose procurement details (pp.10–11).
• EFF: Publish all tenders, blacklist wrongdoers, conduct lifestyle audits of officials, and make merit-based appointments (pp.26, 60).
• RISE (national): End cadre deployment, create an independent anti-corruption agency, and protect whistleblowers (pp.15, 18).
• VF Plus: Appoint on merit, make Municipal Public Accounts Committees function, disclose contracts, and prosecute accountable officers (pp.18–19, 29).
Crime, safety and security
• ActionSA: Grow municipal law enforcement, build specialised units against cable theft and similar crimes, and use municipal powers against illegal immigration (pp.3, 40).
• ANC: Run Community Safety Forums, strengthen municipal law enforcement, and produce a camera and lighting plan per hotspot. It notes policing is mainly SAPS's job (pp.14, 32–33).
• DA: Devolve policing powers to capable municipalities, adopt plural policing, and support neighbourhood watches and technology (pp.28–30).
• EFF: Strengthen by-law enforcement, set up municipal courts, and deploy CCTV, drones and gunshot detection (pp.59–60).
• RISE (national): Professionalise SAPS, add detectives and forensics, and create special courts (pp.23–25).
• VF Plus: Coordinate police, community policing forums, neighbourhood watches and private security, and add CCTV and streetlights (p.21).
Land reform and economic transformation
• ActionSA: Upgrade informal settlements, speed up title deeds and support site-and-service. No dedicated land-reform section was found (p.56).
• ANC: Publish a municipal land and property register within 100 days, release land for housing and enterprise, and pursue spatial transformation (pp.14, 23, 29).
• DA: Release state land for housing, deliver title deeds, and amend the PIE Act on land invasions (pp.47–48).
• EFF: Stop selling municipal land, expropriate abandoned land and buildings, and redistribute municipal land to black communities (p.18).
• RISE (national): Land distribution with lawful expropriation where necessary (p.44).
• VF Plus: Protect property rights, and base empowerment on socio-economic circumstances rather than race (pp.16, 23).
Healthcare and public services
• ActionSA: Extend clinic hours where municipalities run them, and add mobile and outreach services. It notes primary healthcare is mainly provincial (pp.58–59).
• ANC: Strengthen environmental health services and increase HIV prevention access through clinics (p.35).
• DA: Press for proper funding of municipal clinics, standardise clinic procedures and introduce digital dispensing (p.36).
• EFF: Expand primary care with mobile units, longer clinic hours and digital patient systems (p.38).
• RISE (national): Convert clinics into primary care and health promotion centres, and add mental health services (pp.44, 50).
• VF Plus: No dedicated healthcare commitments. The closest are fire-fighting and disaster management (pp.21–22).
Education and access to opportunities
• ActionSA: No dedicated commitments. It has transport and skills-related items (pp.29, 57).
• ANC: Sanitation in every public school and clinic, and free public Wi-Fi at libraries (pp.23–24).
• DA: After-school sport, arts and culture programmes, plus internships and apprenticeships (pp.11–12, 30).
• EFF: Apprenticeships and youth digital-skills hubs (pp.29–30).
• RISE (national): A basic education reform roadmap, eradicating pit latrines, and a National Youth Service for one million young people (pp.51–52).
• VF Plus: No dedicated commitments.
